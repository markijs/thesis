Generous acts of kindness and practical support have carried this undertaking forward. To those who offered their time, counsel and encouragement during my doctoral research, I am profoundly grateful.

First and foremost, I wish to acknowledge Axel Kuhn for his patient mentorship, invaluable contributions and for the enriching opportunities to work abroad. My thanks also go to Thorsten Peters and Thomas Halfmann for their incredible hospitality and guidance during my secondment in Darmstadt. I wish to extend my gratitude to the entire LIMQUET European Training Network for its generous support and for fostering such a collaborative community. I am grateful to all the students within the network for their friendship and exchange of ideas. Special thanks is due to Thomas Barrett and Ben Yuen, whose guidance and encouragement, particularly in the early years of my PhD, were instrumental to my development as a researcher. I am especially grateful to Ezra Kassa, who, apart from being a postdoctoral colleague, has also been a true friend. He has always reminded me to meet challenges with composure and good humour. I would also like to thank Juan Rafael Álvarez, who began this journey alongside me and with whom I shared many defining PhD moments. His companionship and talent for bringing people together were truly admirable. I am grateful to Thomas Doherty for his collaboration on the cavity project, the many technical discussions and his shared enthusiasm for (over-)engineering. My thanks go to Jan Ole Ernst for our sustained and productive discussions on atom–photon interactions and for the mutual encouragement we offered one another, in our research and on the runs we shared. To Chloe So and Matthew King, I offer my thanks for making the Atom-Photon Connection group such a warm and uplifting place.

Beyond the immediate research group and collaborations, many others have played an important part in supporting me throughout this journey. I would like to begin by thanking the Exeter College Chapel Choir, which introduced me to Anglican choral music and to a wonderful community of people whose companionship---especially in our shared singing---has been a continuous source of joy throughout these years. I am especially grateful to my fellow bass Adam Rebick for his friendship and for the many adventures we undertook together; those escapes from the PhD were much needed and greatly appreciated. I would also like to thank Caitlin Harrison and Joseph Crane---their warm friendship, patient teaching and the many hours we spent making music together have been invaluable. Their devotion to music continues to inspire me.

I have also been fortunate to meet many wonderful friends at university, both in Delft and Oxford, whose companionship has brought joy, encouragement and a much-needed sense of perspective during this journey. My heartfelt thanks go to Jaap Wesdorp, with whom I have grown since our earliest days at university. I was honoured to serve as paranymph at his PhD defence ceremony. Despite the distance between us, his friendship, encouragement and support have been a lasting source of strength. I would like to acknowledge Pascal van den Bosch for his warm generosity, thoughtful advice and for inspiring much of this thesis’ typesetting and design; I can only hope my TikZ diagrams reflect his high standard. My sincere thanks also go to my friend Oliver Karnbach, whose companionship during the pandemic was a true gift. Our countless cycling adventures left little unexplored and will certainly be missed. The insight from our lengthy conversations about life remains influential to this day.

I am deeply grateful to Georgios Vacalis for his loyal and unfailing friendship. From college lunches to countless gym sessions, we made many lasting memories. Through both light-hearted moments and more difficult periods, his steadiness, good humour and practical support have been truly meaningful. From the first week of the doctorate, Burcu Gülen has been my constant companion. We endured setbacks and celebrated every small victory together---even an approved extension was for us an excellent reason to celebrate. Her presence transformed what might have been a solitary ordeal into a worthwhile experience that I can look back on with gratitude and a smile. I am forever thankful she walked this road with me. I cannot thank Bernadette Urtz enough for her unfailing support and for welcoming me into her home. Without her extraordinary generosity this PhD would quite simply not have been possible. Her understanding, practical care and steady kindness during the hardest stretches were invaluable to me. I remain profoundly grateful for all she has done.

My family may have been far away, but in my heart and mind they have always been beside me, supporting and encouraging me every step of the way. Their quiet faith in me gave me strength through the harder days. I hope I have made them proud and thank them with all my heart.
