{\Large\sc Abstract}
\vspace{1.3em} % identical explicit skip before paragraph text
\end{center}
%\startsquarepar
Coherent and scalable coupling of single atoms to optical resonators is fundamental to the distribution of entanglement across quantum networks. In this thesis, we present the development of optical dipole trapping techniques and a compatible architecture for a quantum interface between atoms and photons using a high-finesse Fabry--Pérot resonator. We demonstrate enhanced loading of single-atom dipole traps beyond the collisional-blockade limit through modulation of the trap depth, achieving a time-averaged single-site occupancy of \SI{79\pm 2}{\percent}. To realise reconfigurable and scalable trap arrays, we benchmark hologram generation algorithms, establishing weighted Gerchberg--Saxton and its combination with mixed region amplitude freedom as optimal in terms of achieving array uniformity and optical power efficiency. We further present a Fabry--Pérot resonator with a finesse of $\num{7.51(9)e4}$, capable of reaching a single-atom cooperativity of \num{10.4} and thus enabling operation in the strong-coupling regime. Through finite-element modal response analyses we develop a mechanically stable cavity mount compatible with ultra-high-vacuum environments and a pyramidal MOT as an integrated cold-atom source. Together, these results establish a robust and scalable platform for integrating reconfigurable dipole-trap arrays with high-finesse optical resonators, advancing the experimental groundwork for network-ready light-matter interfaces.
