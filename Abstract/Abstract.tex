\vspace{2em}
{\Large\sc Abstract}\\[2em]

\end{center}
\startsquarepar
This thesis presents the development of optical dipole trapping techniques and cavity-based architectures for interfacing single neutral atoms with high-finesse Fabry--Pérot resonators. Such systems form key components for quantum networks, where coherent atom–photon coupling enables long-distance entanglement distribution and quantum information processing. We demonstrate enhanced single-atom loading of microscopic dipole traps beyond the collisional-blockade limit through dynamic modulation of the trap depth, achieving time-averaged single-site occupancies exceeding 80 without the need for rearrangement. Hologram generation techniques for spatial light modulators are benchmarked, establishing weighted Gerchberg–Saxton and hybrid iterative algorithms as optimal for producing uniform, reconfigurable trap arrays. A Fabry--Pérot resonator with a finesse of is designed, constructed, and characterised, yielding a single-atom cooperativity of 10.4 and operation in the strong-coupling regime. Finite-element modal analyses inform a mechanically stable cavity mount compatible with ultra-high-vacuum environments and integrated cold-atom sources. A pyramidal magneto-optical trap is fabricated and validated as a compact atom-loading module for intra-cavity trapping. Together, these results establish a robust and scalable platform for integrating reconfigurable dipole-trap arrays with high-finesse optical resonators, advancing the experimental groundwork for network-ready atom–photon interfaces in quantum communication and distributed quantum computing.
